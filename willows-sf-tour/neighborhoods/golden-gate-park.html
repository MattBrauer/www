Golden Gate Park

Urban wilderness bigger than Central Park

01 Oak's introduction

02 History & character

Developed from sand dunes starting in 1870, largely through the vision of superintendent John McLaren who shaped it over 55 years. At 1,017 acres it's significantly larger than Central Park and functions as the lungs of the west side. It hosted the 1894 Midwinter International Exposition.

03 Proposed itinerary

10:00 am
Japanese Tea Garden
Opens at 9am. Go early before the crowds. Sit at the tea house. The moon bridge is the photograph you'll take.
11:30 am
de Young Museum
The permanent collection is underrated. The rooftop observation tower has the best interior view of the park.
1:30 pm
Lunch at the Academy Café or picnic on the Music Concourse
The Concourse is a remarkable urban space — surrounded by museums, usually with something happening.
2:30 pm
Stow Lake
Rent a rowboat or just walk the perimeter. Huntington Falls is the only artificial waterfall in a US city park.
4:00 pm
Walk west toward the bison paddock
The park changes character completely. By the time you reach the bison you feel genuinely far from the city.
5:00 pm
End at Ocean Beach
Walk out to the water. Watch the surfers. Feel the wind.

04 Recommended spots

Japanese Tea GardenGarden
Oldest public Japanese garden in the US, dating to 1894. Go early.
de Young MuseumMuseum
Strong American art collection. The rooftop tower is free and worth it alone.
California Academy of SciencesMuseum
Aquarium, planetarium, and natural history under a living roof. Book ahead.
Stow LakePark
Rowboat rentals available. The island in the middle has a waterfall.
Conservatory of FlowersGarden
Victorian greenhouse from 1879. The tropical room is remarkable in winter.
Bison PaddockExperience
In the far western end. A genuinely strange and delightful thing to encounter in an urban park.
